# Case Card - Case_005 Coffee Online Accompanied Immersion

## 1. Basic Info

- Case ID: Case_005
- Project type: coffee consumer understanding; beverage-category mapping; coffee journey; channel-role study; online accompanied immersion + IDI.
- Category scope: total beverages first, then coffee formats such as soluble, RTD, liquid concentrate, drip, beans/powder, capsule and out-of-home coffee.
- Main method: one-week online accompanied immersion covering both weekdays and weekends; real-time researcher interaction; selected participants proceed to IDI.

## 2. Core Research Logic

This project does not treat coffee as a single linear upgrade journey. Consumers may use several coffee formats in parallel for different needs and occasions, while coffee also competes with other beverages.

The design must therefore answer:

1. What role does coffee play within the respondent's total beverage repertoire, and when is another beverage the better solution?
2. What does each coffee format solve in real life, and how is it prepared, paired, bought, evaluated, switched, expanded, upgraded, reduced, or dropped?
3. How did the respondent's coffee knowledge and actual drinking habits develop over time?
4. What roles do information channels and purchase channels play in discovery, trial, stock-up, repeat purchase, and format choice?

## 3. Recommended Module Structure

Use this structure when the Proposal explicitly calls for online accompanied immersion. Do not treat it as a fixed template for every coffee project.

1. **About Me / Welcome to My Home**: life stage, work/study, interests, social life, living/working space, and city context. City and lifestyle are explanatory context, not merely recruitment labels.
2. **My Typical Day**: separate weekday and weekend rhythm; identify drinking moments, state changes, activities, people, and places.
3. **My Beverage Family Portrait**: cover stocked beverages, impulse/on-the-go purchases, and self-made drinks. For coffee, distinguish recurring staples, current favourites, recent trials, and formerly loved but neglected products.
4. **My One-Week Diet & Beverage Diary**: one primary diary captures total beverage choices, food pairing, context, need, preparation, channel, and outcome. Coffee receives a conditional deep dive within the same record whenever it appears; do not default to two independent mandatory diary streams.
5. **My Coffee Journey**: split into `Coffee I Know` (knowledge/mental-model journey) and `Coffee I Drink` (actual use journey).
6. **My Information & Purchase Channels**: separate discovery/seeded information channels from purchase channels and state each channel's role by format.

## 4. Key Question Logic

### Beverage family portrait

- Start from the respondent's total beverage system before asking about coffee.
- Capture where beverages are kept and why; include home, workplace, bag/car or other relevant spaces.
- Differentiate products the respondent stocks, buys on the go, and makes themselves.
- Use relation labels such as staple, favourite, new trial, and neglected product rather than a flat inventory.

### One-week diary

- The root task is a real beverage moment, not a coffee-only event.
- Record: trigger and context; what was chosen; why this option won; preparation or food pairing; acquisition channel when relevant; before/after feeling; and realistic alternatives.
- If coffee appears, follow the coffee branch: format, brand/product, preparation method, amount or duration when meaningful, expected role, experience result, and whether coffee was replaceable in that moment.
- If coffee does not appear in a coffee-relevant moment, ask what prevented it and what became the substitute.
- Keep the respondent-facing task light: one representative daily record plus additional noteworthy moments, unless the Proposal explicitly requires every event.

### Coffee journey

`Coffee I Know` should cover the earliest impression of coffee and how the respondent's information library expanded across formats, drink types, brands, flavours, people, content, and lived experiences. It should not be reduced to a generic brand-impression question.

`Coffee I Drink` should cover first cup, taste adaptation, habit formation, interruption or decline, format/category expansion, brand switching, trading up/down, and occasion shifts. Ask for concrete turning points rather than assuming a simple progression from basic to premium coffee.

### Channels

- Information channels: people, creators, platforms, stores, events, content types, and what makes the respondent notice or trust a new coffee product.
- Purchase channels: where each format is bought, cadence/stock-up logic, channel-specific triggers and barriers, and what would happen if that channel disappeared.
- Screenshots, photos, or order records can be invited as optional supporting material unless the Proposal/platform explicitly makes them mandatory.

## 5. Brand Scope Rule

- When a Proposal says brand equity/power is not a primary objective, do not add a default target-brand evaluation module.
- Target-brand questions may appear only after real usage, category, journey, and channel context is established, and only when necessary to answer an explicit business question.
- Do not let a target-brand question replace the broader category, format, or substitution logic.

## 6. Diary vs IDI Split

- Diary: real beverage and coffee moments, product/format details, preparation, pairing, scenes, immediate reasons, channel traces, and before/after experience.
- IDI: unresolved turning points, subtle taste/quality perception, complex journey interpretation, typology hypotheses, and cross-record contradictions.

## 7. Do Not Overgeneralize

- Do not apply the one-week coffee structure to every beverage project without Proposal support.
- Do not assume all respondents follow a linear coffee upgrade journey.
- Do not require a separate daily coffee submission if one integrated beverage diary can capture the coffee branch.
- Do not turn category scope into a long closed checklist; use the diary and family portrait to reveal the respondent's actual repertoire first.
- Do not expose a specific client brand early merely because it is named in the Proposal.

## 8. Best Reference For

- Coffee portfolio and subcategory role studies.
- Beverage substitution and demand-scenario mapping.
- Coffee journey, habit formation, upgrading/downgrading, and category expansion studies.
- Online accompanied immersion projects that lead into IDIs.
